Contrast checker

Type two colours and see whether they meet WCAG 2.2. It measures the real ratio rather than the rounded one, which is the difference between a page that passes an audit and one that only looks like it does.

— contrast ratio

    What the numbers mean

    WCAG 2.2 asks for at least 4.5:1 for body text and at least 3:1 for large text — 24px, or 18.66px when bold. Borders, icons, focus rings and chart strokes are covered separately by SC 1.4.11, which also wants 3:1. AAA raises the text figures to 7:1 and 4.5:1.

    The word doing the work is at least. A ratio of 4.4986 displays as 4.50 and fails, and most checkers will show you the rounded figure and a green tick beside it. This one compares the unrounded value, shows the rounded one, and says so plainly when the two disagree.

    Alpha

    A semi-transparent colour is not the colour you typed — the browser blends it into whatever is behind it before painting a single pixel. Pass an rgba() or an eight-digit hex and it is composited first, so the ratio is the one a visitor actually sees.

    Your colours never leave the page

    Everything here is arithmetic done in your own browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and the tool makes no network requests at all.
